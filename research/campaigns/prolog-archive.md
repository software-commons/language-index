# Prolog and PARLOG archive investigation

The investigation inspected eight existing discovery leads. It used the pinned Linguist entry and seven exact Computer History Museum origins as discovery evidence. Separate Codex executions directly reacquired five source PDFs and checked the identity, lineage, and technical claims against the exact passages.

| Lead | Reviewed scope |
| --- | --- |
| Prolog | The language identified in Colmerauer and Roussel's November 1992 manuscript. The abstract distinguishes its preliminary and final versions. |
| Preliminary Prolog and Final Prolog | Two historical Marseille editions in the designers' description. The exact years remain open because the abstract and the detailed account use different dates. The archive's numeric labels remain discovery provenance. |
| Prolog IV | A scoped Prolog dialect in the index. The PrologIA manual documents its constraint-programming design, Marseille lineage, and distinction from Prolog III. |
| PARLOG | The language described by Clark and Gregory. The archive heading also covers nearby project names, which retain separate discovery questions. The first page of the paper was inspected as an image because the scan lacks extractable text. |
| Prolog-X and MProlog | Supporting implementations identified within the cited Clocksin and Szeredi records. The evidence establishes no version-wide equivalence to other implementations. |
| NIP | The archive documents the name and its separate release and manual records. The linked Rutherford Appleton Laboratory source report could not be acquired. An accessible original report or manual is needed to qualify the relationship to Prolog-X. |

The original Prolog and Prolog IV identity claims retain their inconclusive dispositions. Their first citations omitted the passages needed to support the full statements. Independently checked revision 2 records cite the complete supporting passages. This batch leaves later ISO Prolog editions, other implementations, and the remaining applicable profile dimensions open.
