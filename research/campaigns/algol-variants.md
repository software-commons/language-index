# ALGOL W, EULER, and Atlas Autocode

Three original Computer History Museum catalog leads map to the languages described in their underlying primary sources. The source of the catalog associations is the dated November 8, 2026 catalog representation. Its organizing headings preserve access to several implementations and reports. The technical claims cite the original language descriptions.

- ALGOL W findings distinguish the Wirth and Hoare proposal from the implemented language described by Stanford. The 1969 and 1972 publications retain their separate dates, authority, and compiler context. [Original catalog entry](https://softwarepreservation.computerhistory.org/ALGOL/algol60impl.html#ALGOL_W)
- EULER findings apply to the 1965 Wirth and Weber design in Stanford CS-20. The report’s EULER IV source listing has separate implementation scope. Two archives returned the same original report bytes after the Stanford route timed out. [Original catalog entry](https://softwarepreservation.computerhistory.org/ALGOL/algol60impl.html#EULER)
- Atlas Autocode findings use the authors’ Manchester manuals. Edinburgh’s October 1965 report and June 1966 amendment bound the KDF9 compiler context separately. [Original catalog entry](https://softwarepreservation.computerhistory.org/ALGOL/algol60impl.html#Atlas_Autocode)

[Lead decisions](../../sces/artifacts/algol-variants-origin-review.json) bind the original candidate rows to scoped identities. [Independent source witnesses](../../sces/artifacts/algol-variants-source-witnesses.json) preserve the exact document and page coordinates.
