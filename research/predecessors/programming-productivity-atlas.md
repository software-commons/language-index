## Identity reconciliation

The 319-root historical Atlas benchmark describes a measurement-oriented population. The overlay distinguishes its coverage universe from measurement admission. Neither population establishes completeness for Language Index. Regex Conformance discovers ecosystems for evaluating regex facilities. The Atlas's original reconstruction does not reproduce the historic 1,025-root Regex Conformance ledger.

The records support a set of identity questions for independent primary-source research.

- **Language and implementation.** Track Python and JavaScript independently of the implementations, engines, and runtime environments that execute them. A single language can have multiple implementations with distinct behavior and applicability.
- **Multiple languages on one facility.** Kotlin, Scala, and Clojure can share a regex facility or runtime environment and still require independent language authority research. Classify evidence at its own level of authority.
- **SQL authority boundaries.** Investigate an ISO SQL standard, individual governed dialects, editions, and vendor implementations as distinct objects where the evidence establishes those roles. A measurement bucket spanning multiple dialects cannot establish a canonical identity or supply allocations to them.
- **Visual Basic lineages.** Treat the historical lineages and the common label as separate reconciliation questions. Look to governing artifacts and release histories for boundaries, aliases, and edition assignments.
- **Underobserved ecosystems.** Preserve candidates from proprietary, enterprise, legacy, embedded, and other sources poorly served by public-code instruments. Measurement eligibility establishes the limits of an instrument. Technical interactions and lineage require their own source evidence independently of observed co-usage.
